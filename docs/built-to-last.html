The rules change, not your way of working

Clavarium sits on top of each official app and uses it as a person would, at its keyboard.

  • A vendor changes its terms, prices or plans? You choose: stay, or move.
  • Moving is one setting, in the same conversation.

InstallSecurity

You decide what goes out

Whatever AI you pick, you stay master of your mail, your calendar, your files and what is sent.

  • The AI vendor gets no access of its own to them: no account link, no key.
  • It receives only what the AI reads for the task at hand, through Clavarium, as you allowed.
  • A mail or a code push goes out through Clavarium: you approve it, or a rule you set decides.
  • The key that pushes code and your mail passwords stay sealed on your server.

For the technical reader

What runs where

Each assistant runs in its own ordinary Ubuntu virtual machine (Multipass), on your server. A plain shell too.

What Clavarium sets up in the app

What you would do by hand: first-launch answers, a trusted work folder, "act without asking" if you chose that mode, Clavarium's tools as MCP connectors, hooks so it knows when the app is working. The program itself is never modified.

How it talks to the app

It types into the app's terminal: what you write or dictate, and on its own to wake the app when a message arrives or re-send an unread line. It reads the screen to know the app's state, such as "Please run /login". Sign-in: it shows the vendor's link in the chat and types the code you paste.

Questions

Which AIs can I use?

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Mistral Vibe and OpenCode, or a plain shell.

Do I keep my plan?

Yes. Your account, your plan and your quotas stay with the vendor.

Is it open?

Yes, MIT. Your machines are ordinary virtual machines, and your files are on your server.

Good to know

  • Each vendor runs its own service and sets its own terms.
  • Your data: the AI reads only what you gave it access to, and usually only part of it. What it reads goes to its vendor, for the task at hand. The vendor has no access of its own, and nothing continuous.
  • What the AI reads for a task goes to its vendor. Most vendors keep it only for a limited time, and let you choose whether it may be used to train their models; on business and API plans, training is generally off.
  • When you switch AI, the conversation, its files and its accesses carry over. What the previous AI kept in its own working session stays with it, ready if you switch back.
  • Changing AI needs a computer of its own (not yet when several AIs share one).
  • An app that changes its screens may need a Clavarium update.
  • A key you give an AI is inside its computer while it runs.
