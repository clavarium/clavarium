Who gets what, and flows

One screen to see and change every right. One place apart to trace, and to carry a whole setup in a file.

Where it lives now (menu in three places, agreed 15-10): the page has three items at the bottom left -- a bar along the bottom on a phone -- Conversations, Access, Settings. Access is one page in three tabs: Map (this screen, first), Devices (once "Devices & sharing"), Assets. Flows is a Map / Flows switch inside Map; Export and Import stay in the header of Access, whatever the tab. A conversation's Access and Sharing chapters open it filtered on that conversation ("Attach an asset", "Share with a device"); a badge takes the filter away. On a phone a box touched rises from the bottom with its links and Edit. Old addresses (?open=links, flows, sharing, assets) lead to their tab.

Who gets what

  • Made, changed, taken away here with the requests of Access, Sharing and Assets, and their confirmations (a second writer next to automatic sending, automatic sending itself on the link, taking back the last thing of a device).
  • Colours: terminal, chat, terminal + chat, read, read and write, use. Dashed: a device to an asset; dotted: waits for something to be added.
  • On a phone: a list, then one box with its links as rows of buttons.

Flows

  • Not shown by default: reached from the Map / Flows switch of Access, or from a box's card (Trace it).
  • Each step also in one sentence; on a phone, the sentences only.

A setup in one file

  • In the file: conversations (tool of tools.json, memory, disk, chat, network, wake-ups with their texts, answerer, sign-in mailbox without password), folders (empty), repositories (address, the commit they are at, how they are reached, never a key), mailboxes (without password), secrets (names), links and their rights, automatic sending on a link, devices (to pair), shortcuts, names of flows.
  • Never: a file's content, a value, a key, a token, a password, a pairing, a time.
  • Waits, drawn dashed and exported again as it came: a secret without value, a mailbox or a private repository without its way in, a device to pair. Done by itself when a secret, mailbox or repository of that name is added; a device by "Pair it", which makes the usual one-use code.
  • The setups of the site (format 1) are read too: a person is a device to pair, an agent a conversation, an asset an empty folder, a key a secret's name; a script is said, not made.
